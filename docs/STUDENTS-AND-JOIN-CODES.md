# Students, join codes, and the phone experience

This guide explains the student side of Study Hub: how students get in, what
they can see, and the tools they have on a phone. No technical knowledge is
needed to follow along.

## How a student gets in (join codes)

Students do not have accounts and do not sign in. Instead, you give them a
**join code** — a short code like `K7PM-2X4R` — and they type it once at the
site's join page. The phone then remembers them for 90 days.

To create a join code:

1. Open your course and click **Student access (join codes)** at the top.
2. Click **Create code**. You can optionally give it a label (for example
   "Class 9B, term 1"), an expiry (in days), and a maximum number of uses.
3. The code is shown **once**. Copy it and share it with your class the way
   you normally share information (write it on the board, say it aloud, put
   it in your class group). Only a scrambled copy is stored — nobody can
   look a code up later, including us.

A code can be **revoked** at any time from the same page; students who used
it lose access immediately. When a code reaches its expiry date or its
maximum number of uses, it stops working by itself.

**Who can create codes:** the course owner or an administrator.

## What students can and cannot see

A student who joined your course sees:

- **Published** material only. Drafts and notes waiting for review are
  invisible to them — they cannot even find out those pages exist.
- **Only your course.** A student joined to Course A cannot open Course B's
  pages; the site answers with a plain "not found".
- Files you attached, if you marked them as visible to students, and a
  **Download** button if you marked them as downloadable. Both flags are
  per file and you set them when you upload (or edit) an asset.
- Google Drive and web links open in a new browser tab.

## Suggestions ("open questions")

Every note has a **Suggest a correction** button. A student can report a
mistake without giving any name — no name, no email, nothing personal is
stored. Suggestions appear on your course page in the **Open questions**
box, with the note they refer to. Click **Mark as handled** when you have
dealt with one.

To keep the site safe from abuse, a device can send at most 3 suggestions
per hour.

## On a phone

The whole site is designed for phones first. Students can:

- **Install the app** — on Android, the site offers an "Add to home screen"
  prompt; on iPhone, open the site in Safari, tap **Share**, then **Add to
  Home Screen**. After that it opens like an app, without the browser bars.
- **Read offline** — the last 20 notes a student opened are saved on the
  device. If the internet drops, the last opened note still opens and reads
  normally. Nothing needs to be done in advance; opening a note is enough.
- **Change the text size** — the A− / A+ buttons in the top bar make all
  text smaller or larger. The choice is remembered on the device.
- **Dark mode** — follows the phone's own light/dark setting automatically.
- **Print a note** — the print view shows just the note, black on white,
  without menus or buttons.
- **Arabic reads correctly** — Arabic titles and note text automatically
  display right-to-left.

## Limits, in plain terms

- Trying join codes: at most **5 tries per minute** per internet address.
  After that the site says "Too many tries" and asks to wait a minute.
- Suggestions: at most **3 per hour** per internet address.
- Download links for files expire after **10 minutes** — that is normal;
  clicking Download again gets a fresh link.

## If something does not work

- A student says their code "does not work": check on the access page that
  it was not revoked, expired, or used up. Then create a new code.
- A student lost their access (new phone, cleared browser): they simply
  enter the same join code again — if it is still valid, they are back in.
- A student reports an error page where a note should be: the note is most
  likely still a draft. Publish it and it appears.
